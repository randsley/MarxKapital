## Summary

MarxKapital is a Julia model of Marxian economics: capital accumulation,
exploitation, the organic composition of capital, the tendency of the rate
of profit, the transformation of values into prices of production, and the
sectoral dynamics of expanded reproduction. It is calibrated on Eurostat
and AMECO data through their official SDMX APIs, and its accumulation
parameters are estimated on the EU sectoral accounts.

- **Status:** functional and tested (639 tests in CI, plus a smoke run of
  every notebook). The default data are synthetic; real data are opt-in.
- **Paper:** `paper/main.tex`, every number generated by the `paper/*.jl`
  scripts. Published on Zenodo,
  [10.5281/zenodo.21705608](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21705608)
  (current version November 2026).
- **Software DOI:**
  [10.5281/zenodo.21705369](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21705369).
- **Website:** <https://randsley.github.io/MarxKapital/>, in English and
  Portuguese, with the documentation and the rendered notebooks.

## What the model does

| Area | What is there | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Core model | Departments with validated invariants; 2 and N departments; technical matrix `A` (random, triangular, block), always productive; ODE integration | `types.jl`, `core.jl` |
| Sectoral coupling (opt-in) | Capital mobility, realization pressure, exact per-sector quantity rationing; gross output reconstructed from the model's state; imported inputs, final demand, netted intermediate replacement with a capital-goods matrix | `core.jl` |
| Values and prices | Labor values, maximum profit rate, Sraffian prices of production, the transformation problem, the wage-profit frontier, a calibration validation score | `analysis.jl`, `distribution.jl` |
| Shocks | Wage shocks, base and shock on the same matrix | `shocks.jl` |
| Market prices and growth (opt-in) | Cross-dual dynamics with market prices; balanced-growth rays, their stability, parameter sweeps | `cross_dual.jl`, `growth.jl` |
| Investment behavior (opt-in) | Reinvestment elasticity `η` (power or linear form), reinvestment schedule, investment floor, exit of unprofitable capital, exit friction, mobility of variable capital `μ_v` | `core.jl` |
| TSSI | Temporal single-system valuation over trajectories; TSSI-driven dynamics with book values at historical cost (opt-in) | `tssi.jl` |
| Automatic differentiation and bifurcations | Exact Jacobians and Hessians, through the rationing fixed point too; continuation of fixed points and of balanced growth (BifurcationKit); Hopf points, limit cycles, Lyapunov exponents, hysteresis | `differentiation.jl`, `bifurcation.jl` |
| Estimation | Capital mobility `μ`, the investment function (`η`), the investment floor `θ`, exit, incremental rates of profit, `μ_v` and `σ_v`, on panels with fixed effects and Driscoll–Kraay errors | `mobility_estimation.jl` |

## Data

- **Eurostat (opt-in):** 19 NACE sections, aggregated to 10 or 3; the real
  matrix `A` from the symmetric input-output table (`NAIO_10_CP1700`,
  domestic use), with final demand and import coefficients from the same
  table; capital stock as reported or by perpetual inventory (one rate per
  sector, or asset by asset); the self-employed imputed per person or per
  hour; market sectors only; `e` over several years.
- **AMECO (opt-in):** every country in the database, through the official
  SDMX API; three valuations of the time series (mixed current-price,
  GDP-deflated, current cost, the reference); the self-employed imputed;
  the Commission's forecast years dropped.
- **Static extracts, offline:** `data/ameco_eu27.csv` (notebooks 7–8, paper
  Section 6.1) and `data/eurostat_eu_sectors.csv` (notebooks 9–14, paper
  Section 5.8), each rebuilt by its script in `data/`.
- **Defaults:** synthetic data and fixed sector weights, so that the tests
  run offline.

## Notebooks

Fourteen Jupyter notebooks, committed executed (`notebooks/jupyter/`):

1–6, the model: accumulation, values and prices, calibration and shocks,
coupled sectors and automatic differentiation, TSSI valuation, growth,
cycles and chaos. 7–8, the EU from AMECO: profit rates across the EU27 and
capital mobility between countries. 9–14, the EU sectoral panel: sectoral
mobility in Portugal and the EU, reinvestment, incremental rates of profit,
the investment floor with market prices, and the mobility of variable
capital with TSSI-driven dynamics.

Three Pluto notebooks (`notebooks/pluto/`) explore accumulation, cycles
and the wage-profit frontier interactively.

## Testing

`test/runtests.jl` (639 tests) runs offline, locally and in CI (GitHub
Actions, Julia 1.10 and 1, on every push and pull request to `main`). CI
also smoke-runs every notebook (`test/notebooks.jl`). The network paths
(`data_source = :eurostat`, `sector_data = :eurostat`, `method = :sdmx`)
are checked by hand with `check_ameco_sdmx()`, `check_eurostat_sectoral()`
and `paper/matrix_audit.jl`.

## Still open

The full list, with status per item, is the table at the top of
[`docs/LIMITATIONS.md`](LIMITATIONS.md). The main points:

- The netted reproduction balance holds with real tables only for a few
  years, because nothing in the coupled system keeps sectoral proportions
  in line with `A`; market prices, which would, cannot be combined with
  rationing.
- Rationing converges locally (proved), but global convergence and
  uniqueness of the fixed point are not proved.
- The accumulation cycles appear only far outside the estimated range of
  `μ_v`; chaos rests on numerical evidence.
- Some sectors have `e ≤ 0` in the data, which the model does not allow,
  so their `e` is floored.
- Input-output tables exist only for benchmark years, and imported inputs
  are not available in the cross-dual model or the TSSI layer.

## Roadmap

- A mechanism that keeps sectoral proportions in line with `A` under
  rationing (demand-led variable capital, or market prices combined with
  rationing).
- Medium-term extensions: financialization (credit and fictitious
  capital), ecology (energy and material flows), unequal exchange between
  economies, and heterogeneous firms within sectors.
